How the radar works

Implied value — an ensemble of three robust curves fitted to the snapshot's own traded protocols. Each member fits log market cap on log TVL, growth and log chains by Huber-robust least squares (k = 1.345, iterated to convergence) over the traded protocols in this snapshot, excluding exchange tokens, bridges, liquid-staking and wrapped products, stablecoin products and anchored BTC (the same products the watchlist rule excludes), and excluding market-cap-to-TVL ratios outside 0.01–10× as data errors. On this snapshot that is 78 comps. A comp whose residual is an outlier is downweighted instead of bending the curve, so one weird protocol cannot set the price of the whole board. The three members differ in one thing each: the first takes growth raw, the second takes it through a signed log so a +400% week counts little more than a +4% week, and the third adds a TVL-curvature term (log TVL − 17)² so the size curve can bend for the biggest protocols. The board's value is the mean of the three members' log predictions. No member alone beats the previous single-fit model on every backtest number; their average does, because the members disagree most where each is weakest. Each category then gets a shrunk offset (n/(n+20)) from its comps' mean residual, per member: what its traded protocols actually trade at, not a fee-capture guess. No name is ever special-cased and no coefficient is hardcoded: the same code fits any snapshot the house seals, and each detail view recomputes its row from the three fitted members.

What was tested and rejected. Adding the excluded products back (111 comps) lifts R² to 0.446 but wrecks the backtest — median error ×1.84, only 32.4% inside 2× — because bridges, liquid staking and stablecoins do not price like a new launch. Widening the ratio filter to 0.005–20× does the same (median ×1.90). Age, in log form or as buckets, still flips sign across comp sets and still hurts every backtest number, so it stays out of the price. A median (L1) fit alone reaches a log RMSE of 1.504 but its median factor is ×1.82, so it stays out of the ensemble. Every candidate change was tried the same honest way — leave-one-out, bounds, fits and offsets all refitted without the comp being priced — and kept only when it improved all three backtest numbers. Age is still shown on every row and still sets the farm plan's hold time — a young protocol needs longer on record — it just does not price the token, because the traded protocols say it does not.

The band is what the backtest earns. Every comp is priced again by an ensemble that never saw it (leave-one-out, winsor bounds, robust fits and category offsets all refitted, for all three members). About 44% of the comps land within 2× of their real market cap and half within about ×1.72; the fit's R² is near 0.33. That is the honest size of the signal in this snapshot, and each row carries its band: implied value ×/÷ the leave-one-out median factor. A wide band is not a failure of the board — it is the measurement, shown next to the number.

Netted farm plans. Each plan is generated from the protocol's own category, chains, TVL, age and seven-day trend: the capital, the hold time and the weekly cadence follow those figures, the first step names its primary chain and TVL tier, a growing row gets scale-in advice while a shrinking one gets a smaller first deposit until the trend flattens, and every step states what it costs and how long it takes. The detail view nets the plan: the capital is a deposit that comes back on exit, so the true cost is the gas across the plan, shown against the low end of the row's backtest band.

Scope. The watchlist is the floor, never the ceiling: 40 protocols at or above $50M of TVL that record no token and are not a wrapped, staked or bridged product of one that trades. The board adds every other rankable pre-token protocol the snapshot holds — 19 above the floor the watchlist leaves out, and 138 below it — so the radar finds future launches early.

Reading the record. “Records no token” means the snapshot's DefiLlama record carries no market cap, no CoinGecko id and no symbol for the protocol. It is the record, not proof: a protocol may still have a token its record does not carry. The Live tokens tab shows, separately, the protocols whose token already trades, at the market's own value — never mixed into the ranking.

Nothing here is financial advice, and a launch is never promised.
