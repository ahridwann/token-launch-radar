How the radar works

Implied value — a curve fitted to the snapshot's own traded protocols. The model fits log market cap = a + b·log TVL + g·growth + y·age by least squares over the traded protocols in this snapshot, excluding exchange tokens, bridges, liquid-staking and wrapped products, stablecoin products and anchored BTC (the same products the watchlist rule excludes), and excluding market-cap-to-TVL ratios outside 0.01–10× as data errors. On this snapshot that is 78 comps. The fitted slope on log TVL is below 1, so implied value grows slower than TVL: a protocol ten times as big is worth roughly four to five times as much, not ten. Growth is the seven-day TVL change clamped to −50…+150% (null counts as 0), then winsorized to the range the comps cover — about −40% to +3% on this snapshot — and age is years since listing (unknown counts as 1), winsorized the same way: the curve never extrapolates a hot week or a long life beyond anything a traded protocol did, so one +4,448% week cannot multiply a small protocol's value by eight. Both terms are fitted, and both come out small per point — the comps pay almost no premium for a hot week or a long life, and their fitted signs flip across comp sets — so ordinary weeks move a row little. Each category then gets a shrunk offset (n/(n+8)) from its comps' mean residual: what its traded protocols actually trade at, not a fee-capture guess. No name is ever special-cased and no coefficient is hardcoded: the same code fits any snapshot the house seals, and each detail view recomputes its row from the fitted coefficients.

The band is what the backtest earns. Every comp is priced again by a fit that never saw it (leave-one-out, coefficients and category offsets both refitted). Half the comps land within about ×1.8 of their real market cap and about a third within 2×; the fit's R² is near 0.3. That is the honest size of the signal in this snapshot, and each row carries its band: implied value ×/÷ the leave-one-out median factor. A wide band is not a failure of the board — it is the measurement, shown next to the number.

Netted farm plans. Each plan is generated from the protocol's own category, chains, TVL, age and seven-day trend: the capital, the hold time and the weekly cadence follow those figures, the first step names its primary chain and TVL tier, a growing row gets scale-in advice while a shrinking one gets a smaller first deposit until the trend flattens, and every step states what it costs and how long it takes. The detail view nets the plan: the capital is a deposit that comes back on exit, so the true cost is the gas across the plan, shown against the low end of the row's backtest band.

Scope. The watchlist is the floor, never the ceiling: 40 protocols at or above $50M of TVL that record no token and are not a wrapped, staked or bridged product of one that trades. The board adds every other rankable pre-token protocol the snapshot holds — 19 above the floor the watchlist leaves out, and 138 below it — so the radar finds future launches early.

Reading the record. “Records no token” means the snapshot's DefiLlama record carries no market cap, no CoinGecko id and no symbol for the protocol. It is the record, not proof: a protocol may still have a token its record does not carry. The Live tokens tab shows, separately, the protocols whose token already trades, at the market's own value — never mixed into the ranking.

Nothing here is financial advice, and a launch is never promised.
