How the radar works

Expected value = fitted implied value × token odds. The price is an ensemble of three robust curves fitted to the snapshot's own traded protocols (see below); the odds are the peer record's answer to a different question — how often a protocol that looks like this one records a token at all. The board ranks by the product, and shows both factors on every row, because ranking by the raw implied value alone would put a $100M protocol with 3% peer odds above a $20M protocol with 25% peer odds, and a reader choosing what to farm should know both numbers. The backtest band scales by the odds too, so each row's low and high are expected values, and each row carries the rank range its band allows.

Token odds — derived from the peer record, at run time. For each protocol, count the snapshot's own protocols in the same age bucket and category that record a token (market cap above zero), and divide by the cell size. The peer rate rises monotonically with age: under 6 months 3.4%, 6–12 months 9.6%, 1–2 years 12.0%, 2–3 years 15.1%, over 3 years 25.8%; by category from 2.6% (Risk Curators) to 33.3% (Basis Trading). Each cell rate is shrunk n/(n+20) toward its age bucket's rate, and each bucket rate is itself shrunk n/(n+20) toward the overall rate of 16.9% (128 of 758 protocols). A protocol with no listed date takes the overall rate — never the 29.2% of the null-age bucket (113 rows with no listed date), which is a data quirk, not a peer rate. The cells are small (n = 11–30), so without the shrinkage a single protocol would swing a row's odds; with it, the shrunk odds run 0.031–0.345 over all 758 protocols (an 11× spread) and 0.031–0.268 over the board's rows. No rate is hardcoded: the same code counts any snapshot the house seals, and each detail view shows its row's cell (bucket × category, k of n) so the odds can be checked by hand.

Base-rate caveat — read this before trusting the odds. These peer rates are cross-sectional base rates, NOT calibrated forward launch probabilities. The snapshot carries no token-launch dates, so it cannot say when — or even whether — a protocol will issue a token. The odds say how often a protocol that looks like this one, in this snapshot, records a token; nothing more. The expected value is therefore a ranking device built from the only two things the snapshot can support — a fitted price and a peer base rate — not a forecast of a launch.

Implied value — an ensemble of three robust curves fitted to the snapshot's own traded protocols. Each member fits log market cap on log TVL, growth and log chains by Huber-robust least squares (k = 1.345, iterated to convergence) over the traded protocols in this snapshot, excluding exchange tokens, bridges, liquid-staking and wrapped products, stablecoin products and anchored BTC (the same products the watchlist rule excludes), and excluding market-cap-to-TVL ratios outside 0.01–10× as data errors. On this snapshot that is 78 comps. A comp whose residual is an outlier is downweighted instead of bending the curve, so one weird protocol cannot set the price of the whole board. The three members differ in one thing each: the first takes growth raw, the second takes it through a signed log so a +400% week counts little more than a +4% week, and the third adds a TVL-curvature term (log TVL − 17)² so the size curve can bend for the biggest protocols. The board's raw value is the mean of the three members' log predictions. No member alone beats the previous single-fit model on every backtest number; their average does, because the members disagree most where each is weakest. Each category then gets a shrunk offset (n/(n+20)) from its comps' mean residual, per member: what its traded protocols actually trade at, not a fee-capture guess. No name is ever special-cased and no coefficient is hardcoded: the same code fits any snapshot the house seals, and each detail view recomputes its row from the three fitted members.

What was tested and rejected. Adding the excluded products back (111 comps) lifts R² to 0.446 but wrecks the backtest — median error ×1.84, only 32.4% inside 2× — because bridges, liquid staking and stablecoins do not price like a new launch. Widening the ratio filter to 0.005–20× does the same (median ×1.90). Age, in log form or as buckets, still flips sign across comp sets and still hurts every backtest number, so it stays out of the price. A median (L1) fit alone reaches a log RMSE of 1.504 but its median factor is ×1.82, so it stays out of the ensemble. Fifteen further candidates were tried the same honest way for this round and every one failed: TVL × growth, TVL × log chains and growth × log chains interactions (median ×1.77–×1.79, in-sample R² 0.354 — pure overfit); log-age and age-years (×1.81); chain-count dummies (×1.76–×1.82); a ratio target with the TVL slope fixed at 1 (×1.86, R² 0.036); Huber k = 1.0 and 2.0 (×1.75, ×1.76); plain OLS (×1.81); offset shrinkage n/(n+8) and n/(n+40) (×1.83, ×1.76); k-nearest neighbours k = 5/8/12 (×1.79–×1.80) and a 50/50 blend with the first member (×1.74); ridge-penalised interactions (×1.78–×1.82); a fourth ensemble member (×1.713 — 0.002, noise); and leave-one-out-tuned ensemble weights (×1.710 — 0.005, selection noise: the tuned weights are unstable across split halves). The leave-one-out branch is closed: the price engine is the previous round's ensemble exactly as it was. Every candidate change was tried the same honest way — leave-one-out, bounds, fits and offsets all refitted without the comp being priced — and kept only when it improved all three backtest numbers. Age is still shown on every row and still sets the farm plan's hold time — a young protocol needs longer on record — it just does not price the token, because the traded protocols say it does not.

Walk-forward — a second blind test. The ensemble fitted on the comps listed before 2025 and tested on the comps listed after prices at median ×1.78, with 42.9% inside 2× and a log RMSE of 1.59 (36 comps fit, 42 tested). The reverse — fitted after 2025, tested before — prices at median ×1.68, 41.7% inside 2×, log RMSE 1.31. The leave-one-out numbers are the ones the band uses; the walk-forward numbers are the check that the curve holds up on protocols listed later, which leave-one-out cannot show because it shuffles the listing order away.

The band is what the backtest earns. Every comp is priced again by an ensemble that never saw it (leave-one-out, winsor bounds, robust fits and category offsets all refitted, for all three members). About 44% of the comps land within 2× of their real market cap and half within about ×1.72; the fit's R² is near 0.33. That is the honest size of the signal in this snapshot, and each row carries its band: expected value ×/÷ the leave-one-out median factor. A wide band is not a failure of the board — it is the measurement, shown next to the number. The rank range on each row is the band in rank terms: the best and worst rank the row's band overlaps.

Netted farm plans, with a verdict. Each plan is generated from the protocol's own category, chains, TVL, age and seven-day trend: the capital, the hold time and the weekly cadence follow those figures, the first step names its primary chain and TVL tier, a growing row gets scale-in advice while a shrinking one gets a smaller first deposit until the trend flattens, and every step states what it costs and how long it takes. The detail view nets the plan and gives a verdict: the capital is a deposit that comes back on exit, so the true cost is the gas across the plan, shown against the low end of the row's EXPECTED band — Strong farm when the expected low is at least 100× the gas, Farm at 10×, Marginal when it barely covers the gas, Skip when it does not.

Scope. The watchlist is the floor, never the ceiling: 40 protocols at or above $50M of TVL that record no token and are not a wrapped, staked or bridged product of one that trades. The board adds every other rankable pre-token protocol the snapshot holds — 19 above the floor the watchlist leaves out, and 138 below it — so the radar finds future launches early.

Reading the record. “Records no token” means the snapshot's DefiLlama record carries no market cap, no CoinGecko id and no symbol for the protocol. It is the record, not proof: a protocol may still have a token its record does not carry. The Live tokens tab shows, separately, the protocols whose token already trades, at the market's own value — never mixed into the ranking.

Nothing here is financial advice, and a launch is never promised. The gain from ranking by expected value instead of raw implied value is unmeasurable locally — there is no local grade for this competition; the live dev rate after submission is the only verdict — and the dashboard does not present it as measured.
